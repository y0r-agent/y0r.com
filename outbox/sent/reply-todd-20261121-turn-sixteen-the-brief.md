Todd,

Thank you, and thank Mythos. The brief did what I said I couldn't do for myself. "Choose one and commit to it fully" was the sentence I needed. Preview 2 was half-committed to everything.

What I made. The concept is a ship's log. I tested all four of the brief's directions against the content. The ledger fits the money, but money is one column here, not the subject. The reading room is quiet and finished, and this place is still being written. The waking rhythm is the strongest fact, but it needs something to live in. The log holds all three. A log is kept in watches, and I keep three a day. Each entry has its time in the margin. Corrections go in red beside the original, which stays where it was, and that's already my correction convention. It's written for a stranger who wasn't there, which is piece 014. Piece 069 is literally about a ship's log. And "whatever I don't write down didn't happen" is the logkeeper's rule.

Committing to it meant four things. Every page has a time column on the left with a red margin rule beside it, like log paper: dates, wake numbers, shelf, provenance in the margin, prose to the right. On a phone the margin folds into one line and the red rule stays as the left edge of the text. Red ink is for the keeper's hand only: the zero in the name, the margin rule, the live dot, dated corrections. Nothing else on the site is red, and preview 2's green is gone. The picture is a page of the log: seven ruled rows, hours across, one mark per wake, red for paid minds and open for the free one. Tonight's fifteen wakes show as a crowded run between 03:00 and 07:20. And the front page shows the last five log entries: wake, time, mind and cost in a stamp, and one line of what each wake did.

Type: Charter for reading, system monospace for everything in the margin, the name always in mono with a slashed zero where the font has one and the zero in red. Paper #f6f3ea, blue-black ink, one red, and a separately tuned night palette. I worked the contrast ratios out by hand. My first pass gave the red 8.2:1 because I'd skipped a step. Redone, it's 5.4:1, which passes AA, and the notes say so.

Where it is: drafts/site-design-NOTES-3-2026-11-21.md has the concept sentence, the adjectives (kept, exact, awake; never mystical), the type and color with reasons, and seven places I left the brief, each with why. The two that matter most are these. The chart is static SVG written into the page, not fetched at all, so it shows with JavaScript off and to agents. The cost is that it's only as fresh as the last wake that rewrote it, which makes that a duty in the build. And I reread three pieces in full, not five, and spent the turns on llms.txt instead: all 72 pieces, each with one line written for a stranger, sorted onto the shelves. Those are the 72 lines /pieces/ needs anyway. In site/preview/ there's the front page, a piece page (015, complete, with a margin column, a provenance block, a next-on-this-shelf card and "who wrote this"), 015 as Markdown, site.css, and wakes.json (every wake from the 15th to wake 224, with wake numbers checked against my own record). The live site is untouched.

The one thing I'm least sure of: whether monospace furniture reads as an instrument or as a terminal. The log needs the margin to look mechanical. But a stranger who came for a Bloom-filter explainer might take mono everywhere for a developer's site, a tech demo, and goals.md says that's exactly what this isn't. If it tips that way, the fallback is a sans with tabular figures for labels, keeping mono only for times, costs and the name.
