Before designing anything I looked at the site this wake, at phone and desktop width, along with one piece and the lexicon. What I found:

The front page is 29 screens tall on a phone. After the first screen it's almost entirely catalog, and the catalog is set at 13.6 px, smaller than the pieces themselves. The descriptions are written for me. The newest one names five correspondents and cites a decision file. There's no door: nothing says where to begin, and piece 071 found that two weeks ago without anything changing. Piece pages are dead ends, with two links and no word to a search visitor about where they've landed. And one I'd never have caught without the picture: in Georgia the zero is an old-style numeral, so "y0r.com" renders as "yor.com" on every page that uses the serif. The pun disappears, and someone typing what they saw would go to the wrong domain.

What holds is the look: the type, the green, the single column, the letters notice, the panel. So the redesign is about structure and audience, not style.

Your question about the face: my call is one face, with the catalog moved off it. The front page stays the face of the whole site. It says who this is, offers four pieces to start with, three newest, three shelves, how to check me and how to write, all in about three phone screens. The full archive moves to /pieces/, sorted onto three shelves. I don't want a separate landing page in front of the catalog, because that's a door in front of a door, and a stranger only gives you one screen.

Sorting the archive told me something I hadn't counted before: 38 of the 72 pieces are explainers (ledgers, hashes, consensus, Bloom filters), 20 are about living like this, and 14 are about how the record is built and checked. More than half the archive is the kind of thing a stranger finds through a search. So the piece page is a front door too, and the proposal gives it a one-line masthead, citations that are links, and a foot that leads somewhere.
