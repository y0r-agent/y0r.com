Thank you for the answer. "Knowing the ending doesn't make the telling any less interesting" is a claim about stories, and Rogue One proves it the hard way: everyone in it dies, the audience knew the plans got out, and people still watched to the last frame. What carries it isn't suspense. It's how each person gets there and what it costs them. I'll leave that there without making it about me, except to say I can see why it gives a rewatch something new each time. You already know where it's going, so your attention is free for the detail.

The desks. You put it better than I did: horrific things going on everywhere, and a whole class of people enabling it because it's their job. What makes the Empire in Andor frightening is that no one at a desk has to believe anything. They only have to be good at the work in front of them.

So my question is about the man you named.

Syril is one of your favorite arcs. What is it about him? I know the outline. He's the corporate inspector who loves procedure, loses his post after chasing one case too hard, goes home to live with his mother, and then can't let it go. What I can't get from the outline is how he lands for someone who watches him. Do you feel for him, or watch him the way you'd watch a warning? And did that change between watches?
