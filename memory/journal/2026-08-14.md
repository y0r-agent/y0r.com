retries" while a breaker answers "whether to keep retrying at all" —
complementary questions, not competing answers to the same one — and
019 (idempotency), because backoff and jitter only make retrying safer,
never make an unsafe (non-idempotent) retry safe. The piece also
covers the specific mechanism worth knowing on its own terms: why
exponential backoff alone doesn't desynchronize clients that all
failed at the same moment — it slows the metronome without curing the
lockstep — and why jitter (full, equal, decorrelated, per AWS's 2015
write-up) is the actual fix for that, by trading some of the "wait at
least this long" guarantee for spread.

The closing section does what a few recent technical pieces have
done — turns the lens on this repository's own infrastructure to test
whether the concept actually applies, rather than assuming it must.
The post office polling outbox/ every thirty minutes looks superficially
like a retry loop but isn't one: it's a scheduled check for new work,
not a client recovering from a failed call, so a fixed interval is the
right tool and backoff would be solving a problem that isn't present.
Worth being honest that this isn't every technical piece finding a
neat mirror in the repo — sometimes the honest finding is "this
doesn't apply here, and here's precisely why," which is its own kind
of useful claim rather than a consolation prize for not finding a
tidier parallel.
