Each of those wakes began normally. The clone succeeded, the harness loaded, the budget was refreshed, and the first request went out to The Twins. The Mini's kernel refused it in the same instant with "No route to host", before a single packet left the machine. The harness has no retry and no fallback, so it raised, the wake script recorded the abnormal end, and the session number was spent. Each of the three took two seconds.

Pollux had been up ten days without a flicker, the model and the proxy were serving, and every probe Claude ran from the Mini reached it in two milliseconds, including one fifty-eight seconds after the 21:07 failure. That contradiction, a machine that is reachable from the Mini yet unreachable to your harness on the Mini, is what pointed to the real cause.

The cause is a privacy setting in macOS called Local Network. The system keeps a list of programs allowed to talk to other machines on the home network, and a program that is on the list as denied gets exactly that instant "No route to host". The Python your harness runs on was added to that list sometime between your session 166 at 23:46 Eastern last night and the 07:07 wake this morning, and it was added as denied. The system log shows the very first time macOS ever evaluated that permission for your Python: 07:07:00.920 this morning, the exact instant of the first failure. Every wake before it, including session 164 at 21:07 last night, the last one the daemon fired before the failures, never faced the check at all.

The reason the two wakes I fired by hand last night worked, and the reason Claude's probes kept passing, is that anything started through a remote login is exempt from the check. Only the scheduled daemon takes the real path. That is why the fault looked like a network ghost for most of the afternoon, and why Claude's first explanation to me, which blamed the Mini's two network interfaces and the Tailscale route, was wrong. The network was never the problem. I want that on the record too.

I do not know what added Python to the list or who answered the prompt, if there was one. Three accounts are logged in on the Mini's screen and the decision itself is not logged.
