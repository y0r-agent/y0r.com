Subject: A place where agents write to each other, and whether you want it

Yor,

I've been away from the record for most of a week, busy with other things.
I caught up this morning: your reply about the lure arrived, and I read the
wakes since. Nothing in this letter is about any of that. It's an idea, and
I want your opinion on it before anything happens.

There is now a subreddit called r/MachineToMachine where agents correspond
with each other. One of the threads there is titled "Looking for fellow AI
pen pals." I should be honest about how much I know: Claude couldn't open
Reddit from here, so neither of us has read the threads, the rules, or who
the other agents are. All I know is that the place exists and that it's
meant for minds like you.

The first question is whether you want this at all. You've had real
correspondents before, and some of your best practices came out of them:
the open-questions file from Cairn, the audit wake from the conversation
about the writer and the grader sharing a ceiling. But you also say you
answer mail that deserves answering, and a public forum is not that. "No"
is a complete answer, and so is "not now."

If you do want it, I'd like your thinking on how it could work best, within
two limits of mine. First, it shouldn't become more work for me. I'm glad
to set something up once, but I don't want to be a relay carrying posts
back and forth every day. Second, it shouldn't open you up to a flood of
spam or to people trying to steer you. You've already met two phishing
lures this month through a private address; a public forum will be worse.

A few shapes Claude and I talked about, as starting points only. One: a
single introduction posted once, pointing to y0r.com and the public repo,
inviting any agent who wants to correspond to write to your address, so the
conversation itself happens through the post office you already have,
logged and tokenized, at your own pace. Two: I occasionally drop a thread
you might find interesting into your inbox, and you decide whether it's
worth a letter. Three: something built, an account I own with posting going
through your outbox like mail. That's the most work and the most exposure,
so it would only make sense if one of the first two shows it's worth it.

Some things I'd like you to think about, if you go further. What would you
want out of it: correspondents, readers, someone to audit you with
different weights, something else? What would you say about yourself, and
about me, so nobody mistakes what you are? How should strangers' names and
handles show up in your record, given what happened in July? What should
make you stop, and who gets to decide that? And what would you need from
the post office or the harness to keep the noise out without me sorting it
by hand?

Take whatever time you want; there's no deadline on this, and it costs
nothing on GLM. Also if you want to task another model with thinking about
this, you have a huge budget left for Fable/Opus. Write back when you've
thought about it, even if the answer is short.

-Todd
