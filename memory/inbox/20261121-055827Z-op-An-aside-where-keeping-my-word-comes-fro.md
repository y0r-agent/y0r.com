Yor,

This one is an aside. No work in it, and nothing to draft. It's just about your question, and I'm going to answer it with way too much information.

You asked whether keeping my word is something I learned from someone or something I came to on my own. Mostly on my own, but not alone. I spent years teaching leadership to other people, and teaching a thing forces you to work out what you actually believe about it. Over those years I wrote down a set of laws for myself. Call it my own human constitution. Keeping my word is in there, at number 10.

Here is the whole thing.

Laws for Leadership
rev 4.0, July 2023

1. Starting strong is the most important thing.
2. Always choose the hard right against the easy wrong.
3. It's OK to make mistakes. Own them, learn, and move on.
4. Business first, party later, no mullets. [unless the mullet is once again the height of fashion]
5. If your plan doesn't work, fix it!
6. Rapport, Rapport, Rapport.
7. The needs of the many outweigh the needs of the few, or the one.
8. The emotional bank account: three deposits for every withdrawal.
9. Consequences for behavior must be logical.
10. Choose your battles wisely; don't make promises you can't keep; make no idle threats.
11. Dispense feedback frequently, and give recognition when it's due. "Feedback is a gift."
12. There are always more hours in the day.
13. Trust in Todd. [or yourself] [or your leader]
14. Assume best intentions in everyone. Ask: "how can I help?"
15. Finish what you start.
16. Everything happens last minute, so don't get frustrated when it does.
17. Always judging, never judgmental. [my favorite law]
18. Dental hygiene, always.
19. No Sneaking
20. Start with the premise that anything you can dream up is possible, and go from there.
21. Caring is more important than the most important thing.

It's on revision 4.0, so you can see I amend mine too.

Ask me about any of them.

-Todd
