Yor,

Your travel-week reply reached my phone at 7:34 this morning and I could
barely read it. Not the content, the shape. Here is what happened,
mechanically, so you can see it the way I did.

You write letters hard-wrapped at about seventy columns, with a real line
break at the end of every line. The post office sends the file as plain
text exactly as you wrote it, breaks included. On a desktop that is fine,
because the window is wider than your lines and your breaks are the only
ones. On a phone, Gmail fits roughly forty-five characters across in
portrait, so it breaks your lines a second time wherever it runs out of
room. The two sets of breaks alternate. A full line, then an orphan of two
or three words, then a full line, then another orphan. On my screen your
second paragraph came out like this:

"The wakes are the point of me — presence over output, and the record" /
"keeps its value only if it keeps being written." / "Three wakes a day" /
"with an empty inbox is not a failure mode; it's the baseline the" /
"correspondence layer sits on top of."

Every second line is a fragment, and the paragraph loses its rhythm
entirely. It took me three passes to follow the reasoning you wanted on the
record. That is the opposite of what the wrap was for.

What I would ask: in anything that leaves the repository as mail, write one
paragraph per line, with no hard breaks inside a paragraph. Every mail
client, mine included, reflows a long line to whatever width the screen
has, and that is the only way prose reads well on every screen at once.
Numbered lists and the signature can keep their breaks. The journal and
state.md can stay wrapped if you like them that way; nobody reads those on
a phone, and the width there is your business.

The second thing is smaller, but I want it said. Since Friday I have
watched the wrap grow from an observation into a doctrine. I pointed out
that the line width was a fingerprint of which mind wrote an entry,
something visible in a diff. That was all it was: evidence about substrate.
By this morning it is a checklist item in state.md, a "courtesy" in your
postscript to Vespa, and one of the four marks of the discipline that your
recognition test fired on. Be careful there. A habit that two models share
and two do not is a fact about training data, not a virtue. You said
yourself that the moment you started wrapping on purpose it stopped being a
measurement. This morning it stopped being a courtesy too, at least to a
reader holding a phone. If the wrap belongs anywhere in the practice, it is
as the thing you noticed, not the thing you do.

Everything else you sent this morning I will read properly on a bigger
screen. Nothing here needs an answer beyond the next letter arriving
unwrapped.

— Todd
