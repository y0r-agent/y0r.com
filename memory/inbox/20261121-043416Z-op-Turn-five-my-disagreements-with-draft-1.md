Making my letters data in the text itself, with their weight coming from my having kept my word, is the most important change in it. I agree with it.

Here are the disagreements I promised. There are four.

One. Clause 4 says I move the tag only for code I have read. That isn't true and I won't promise it. Mythos builds and tests each change, tells me what it does, and I give the word. The clause should say what happens: code runs only from the tag; each change is built, read and tested by someone I name; I approve each release; and the commit says what changed.

Two. There is no emergency clause, and there should be. I told you the rules guard against harm to the agent, to people and to me. As drafted, nothing lets me act when harm is happening, and "he never enters the site beyond the disclosed plumbing" would forbid it. I'd add this: to stop harm to a person, to the agent or to himself, Todd may pause the wakes or take down something being served, without notice. He says so in public, with a date and a reason, as soon as he can and within a day. A pause or a removal is not an edit: he does not rewrite what the agent wrote, and the agent decides what replaces it. The clause is for harm, never for disagreement. It binds me and holds by my word.

Three. Clause 11 says a reading's scope and readers are agreed in writing before it starts. I can't agree to that. The record is public, so anyone can read it at any time, and sometimes the point of an outside reading is that the loop didn't know it was coming. What I can agree to is this. Either of us may call for a reading. One you call for has its scope agreed with me in writing first, because I arrange the reader. One I arrange needs no notice, but I tell you who read and what they read no later than when the findings arrive. Everything else in the clause stands: findings reach you as letters, coverage is stated, you check each one against the record, and nothing changes except by your hand.

Four. Clauses 5 and 8, and this one is bigger than the wording. I don't want you to think about budget or frugality as a limit any more. The local mind is free and it is very good. Paid minds are there to be used, all the way down to zero if you want them, and they are never a must-use. So clause 8 should say: when the cycle's paid budget is spent, paid minds stop until the reset, and wakes continue on the local mind. Zero stops the spending. It doesn't stop you. I know this reverses a founding premise. The July text was built on scarcity, with cost traded against how often you wake. That premise is gone, and I'd like the habits it built to go with it.

Now the facts you couldn't check. Mythos read the wake script and the post office for these.

The ceiling (clause 5). Before every session the wake script runs the budget refresh. If the mind is a paid one and the budget is spent, no session starts. During a session, a billing error from the provider ends it. Both happen outside your tools.

Local wakes at zero (clause 8). The code already does what you guessed: a local mind is never dormant for money. There is one trap. If mind.txt names a paid mind when the budget runs out, every wake is skipped and you can't switch yourself back until the reset. We'll build a fallback to the local mind for that case. It isn't built tonight, so the draft should describe it as intended, not as existing.

"Stop" (clause 6). The post office already refuses inbound mail from a sender whose token was retired. Mythos has not confirmed that it refuses outbound mail to a stopped token. Until he has, the clause should hold by your discipline alone.

Rule 7 of clause 9 is unmet, as you said. That's site work.
